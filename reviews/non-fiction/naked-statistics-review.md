## Key Frameworks and Models
*List and briefly explain the most important frameworks introduced*

1. **Descriptive Statistics**: 
   - Core concept: Descriptive statistics, like a batting average, gives us a route by which we can condense many traits or information into a single number. This has the advantage of allowing us to rank or compare via a single number or index.
   - Application: Income can be compared via mean, but mean has the color of large variances and extremities. For instance, the mean income in America is $63,000. That number is misleading because income itself has dramatically increased **for the top 90th percentile** and remained stagnant for the rest. A better lense into the issue would be the median, for which income would be **$40,000** for the average individual in the United States. It is also important to recognize that, in a normalized distribution, the standard deviation of income in the united states is $30,000. That would mean you should expect 68% of the individuals you meet in America to make anywhere between $30,000 and $90,000. More practically, on an individual level, 95% of the people you meet will make $120,000 or less. This can also work conversely to demonstrate whatever point you are trying to make in a **deceptive** manner. For instance, the tax cuts given by the Bush administration lowered taxes for 92 million Americans and that the average would be $1000 per household. While that was true, using the mean was deceptive as many of those tax cuts went to the wealthy. The **median** income was actually $100 per household. 

2. **Inferential Statistics**: 
   - Core concept: While descriptive statistics allow us to describe what we observe, inferential statistics allow us to glean information or extrapolate opinions about what we cannot directly observe. 
   - Application: Test takers in Atlanta had scored abnormally well on exams after changes to inventive structure for teachers that placed more emphasis on their students' exam results. The null hypothesis, being that they didn't cheat, would have **normal** erasure rates (meaning erased answers converted to correct ones). However, they found that he erasure rates converted to correct answers were between 20-50 standard deviations above the norm. We can **infer** or surmise from the data, that cheating was occurring. The likelihood of that occurring would be so small that it should happen only once in 1.453 x 10⁸⁶ years.

## Most Valuable Insights
*The 3-5 most important or actionable insights from the book*

1. Seek environments that allow for the chance collision of ideas and tools. 
2. Read far and wide, travel, walk, and place yourself in networks that increase the likelihood of completing your hunches or exploring more of the adjacent possible.
3. Support public platforms like universities or the government so that innovation can be nurtured and grown.
4. Embrace intelligent adjacency by exposing yourself to fields outside your field of expertise. Many solutions come from applying insights from one domain to problems in another.
5. Keep a notebook or journal to track incomplete ideas over time. Other innovations or ideas may help to complete those ideas.
