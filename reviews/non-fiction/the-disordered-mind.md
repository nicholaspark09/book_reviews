# Book Review: The Disordered Mind: What Unusual Brains Tell Us About Ourselves

## Book Information
- **Title**: What Unusual Brains Tell Us About Ourselves 
- **Author**: Eric R. Kandel 
- **Year Published**: 2018
- **Publisher**: Farrar, Straus, and Giroux
- **Genre/Category**: Non-fiction / Science
- **Length**: 254

## Overview

Eric Kandel walks us through some extraordinary cases of brain damage or disease that have revealed how the brain functions and its underlying circuitry. While he goes through some well known examples, I believe this book should be read far and wide by parents, teenagers, and adults because of his demonstration of brain disorders and their various pathologies and treatments; I now understand the potential consequences of hurtful words to a teenager with underlying pregenetic dispositions to depression. Kandel should be praised for having written such an important and insightful story of how the brain works. 

## Summary
*Concise overview of the book's main content and structure (1-2 paragraphs)*

This book discusses how the brain functions and how various diseases and disorders reveal the circuitry in our brains. Autisim demonstrates our innately social nature, how important sections of Chromosome 7 are in our social natures, and how different regions of our brain allow us to form a thought of mind enabling us the ability to predict others' mental states. Depression revealed the role of seratonin in mood regulation, bipolar disorder in how lithium is used by neurons to blunt the overactivation of neurons in manic states. Alzheimer's shows us the way in which our memory works and how the misfolding of proteins regularly used by our neurons, amyloid beta peptide and tau, spreads through the brain ruining our memories.

Kandel concisely summarizes the way in which brain disorders and diseases have shaped our understanding of how the brain functions. As readers, we are left to appreciate the wide scope and complexity in how our brains function as well as the diverse underpinnings of various diseases. The Disordered Mind gives us hope that there will be more accurate and refined treatments for our brains as well as remind us of how much we have left to understand about the brain. It also instills much empathy for those with uncurable diseases such as bipolardisorder or schizophrenia.


## Writing Style and Accessibility
*Assessment of the author's writing quality, clarity, and who can easily engage with this book*

While the author did a good job of breaking down various topics for average readers, some of this book seemed more like a textbook. Due to the various technical terms and quite novel brain structures for a non-medically inclined individual, it may take a bit longer to read than the standard novel. Although it was a longer read than most books, its duration is matched by the utility in learning how the brain functions.
